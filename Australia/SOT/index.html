Day by day

Wed1July

Mount Colah → Jugiong

~385 km · ~4 hrs 15 min · M1 → M31 Hume, all easy towing
Donation camp Flush toilets Riverside Big rig friendly

📍 Jugiong Showground · visitjugiong.com.au

Huge flat grassy showground right on the Murrumbidgee, a few minutes off the Hume. Tidy maintained toilets, picnic tables and BBQs, loads of room to park the van without unhitching dramas. Donation box — drop in what you'd like. Some distant freeway hum, but nothing you'll notice from inside the van.

The charm bit Jugiong punches absurdly above its weight: the Long Track Pantry (jams, chutneys, excellent coffee) and the Sir George — an 1852 coaching inn restored with a restaurant and artisan bakery — are a short stroll from camp. Arrive by 3pm and you'll catch the Pantry before close.
Backup option

Gunning Showground (~75 km earlier) — free unpowered camping for up to 3 nights with toilets and hot showers. Sweet heritage main street with cafés and a pub doing meals. Details on nsw.gov.au

Thu2July

Jugiong → Holbrook

~185 km · ~2 hrs · short day = slow morning + Gundagai stop
Free · 24 hr Toilets Walk to town Submarine

📍 Ten Mile Creek Gardens, Holbrook · council page

Free 24-hour stopover in the parkland behind Holbrook's famous landlocked submarine, HMAS Otway. Toilets in the park, level grass, and the bakery and IGA are a flat stroll up the street. It's popular with grey nomads, so arriving by mid-afternoon still gets you the pick of the spots — and with NSW schools in term until 3 July, it should be a peaceful one.

The charm bit A 90-metre submarine in a paddock 300 km from the sea is peak country NSW. The Submarine Museum is small but genuinely good — original control room, working periscope, a real WWII Jolly Roger — and the Woolpack Inn Museum up the street covers the town's pioneering past. Break the morning drive at Gundagai for the Dog on the Tuckerbox.
Backup option

Holbrook Showground — a few minutes away, cheap powered and unpowered sites with showers if the free camp is chockers or you want a hot-shower night.

Fri3July

Holbrook → Lake Hume

~85 km · ~1 hr · deliberately tiny day — this one's for the view
Free Lakefront BBQ shelter Vans only (no tents)

📍 Ludlows Reserve, Lake Hume · camp guide — ~15 min past Wodonga

Free camp spread along the edge of Lake Hume with big lawns, a covered BBQ/picnic area and water views from the van door. In winter you've a real shot at a spot right on the lake's edge. Caravans and RVs only, no tents — which keeps it quiet and decidedly grown-up. Victorian school holidays will have started, so arrive earlyish for first pick, but the no-tents rule does most of the filtering for you.

The charm bit Sunset over Lake Hume from your annexe, hot drink in hand, halfway point behind you. If you want a leg-stretch en route, cross at Albury and wander Noreuil Park on the Murray. Top up groceries and fuel in Wodonga — it's the last big shop before the final push.
Backup option

Jindera Showground (just NW of Albury) — popular free/donation showground camp with toilets if Ludlows doesn't appeal on the day.

Sat4July

Lake Hume → Mount Moriac

~420 km · ~4¾ hrs · Hume → M80 Ring Rd → Geelong Rd (skips the CBD entirely)
Free with a pub meal Self-contained req'd 1844 country pub 25 min to terminal

📍 Mount Moriac Hotel · mountmoriachotel.com.au — Princes Hwy, just west of Geelong

A historic country pub trading since 1844 that offers free overnight camping in its large carpark for self-contained rigs — exactly what your off-grid van is built for — when you book a table for dinner. Phone ahead in the morning to lock in a table and mention you're camping.

The charm bit Last night on the mainland: a roaring country-pub dinner, no dishes, no packing up a camp kitchen — and a perfect excuse to NOT cook with the fridge you're trying to empty for biosecurity. The terminal is an easy 25-minute run away tomorrow.
Morning detour + backups

Worth the stop: Chiltern is 10 minutes from Ludlows on your way out — a gold-rush town with a National Trust main street that looks like a film set. Coffee and a wander before the big leg.

Backup 1 — Hipcamp "Spirit of Tasmania Stopover", Lovely Banks: a private paddock minutes from the port, made exactly for SOT travellers. Paid, book on Hipcamp.

Backup 2 — Geelong Showgrounds: paid powered sites with showers and a dump point, close to the terminal — pre-booking essential. geelongshowgrounds.com.au

Sun5July

Sailing day · Geelong

Mount Moriac → Spirit of Tasmania Quay, 136 Corio Quay Rd, North Geelong · ~25 min

You've got a whole free day in Geelong before an evening sail. Park up along the waterfront (Eastern Beach has long bays), wander the painted bollards, find a long lunch, and eat down the last of the fresh food. Terminal details: spiritoftasmania.com.au/geelong-terminal

MorningGeelong waterfront — bollard trail, Eastern Beach, a proper long lunch. Empty the fridge of fruit & veg while you're at it.
−3 hrsFinal van check: gas bottles OFF at the valve, no loose jerry cans of concern, e-ticket + number plate details confirmed.
−2½ hrsCheck-in opens. Drive-up window — you stay in the car. Free parking at the quay for rigs waiting to board.
−2 hrsSweet spot to arrive: through check-in and the biosecurity inspection without queue stress.
−1½ hrsBoarding starts. Grab EVERYTHING you need for the night — no returning to the vehicle deck once you sail.
−45 minCheck-in closes. Hard cutoff — don't test it.
EveningSail. Dinner on board, sleep crossing Bass Strait, wake up in Devonport. 🌊
Biosecurity is the big one: no fruit, vegetables, plants, honey, fish or fish products can go to Tasmania. They inspect at Geelong before boarding (eskies and van fridges get checked), so plan meals from Friday onward to run the fresh stuff down to zero.
