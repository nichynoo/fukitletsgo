Day by day

Wed1July

Mount Colah → Jugiong

~385 km · ~4 hrs 15 min · M1 → M31 Hume, all easy towing
Donation camp Flush toilets Riverside Big rig friendly

📍 Jugiong Showground · visitjugiong.com.au

Huge flat grassy showground right on the Murrumbidgee, a few minutes off the Hume. Tidy maintained toilets, picnic tables and BBQs, loads of room to park the van without unhitching dramas. Donation box — we dropped in a few dollars. Some distant freeway hum, but nothing we noticed from inside the van.

The charm bit Jugiong punches absurdly above its weight: the Long Track Pantry (jams, chutneys, excellent coffee) and the Sir George — an 1852 coaching inn restored with a restaurant and artisan bakery — are a short stroll from camp. Arriving by 3pm meant we caught the Pantry before close.
Backup option

Gunning Showground (~75 km earlier) — free unpowered camping for up to 3 nights with toilets and hot showers. Sweet heritage main street with cafés and a pub doing meals. Details on nsw.gov.au

Thu2July

Jugiong → Beechworth

~350 km the scenic way — Gundagai, Batlow and the Bethanga Bridge
Powered site Campfires OK Creekside 1.5 km to town

📍 Beechworth Holiday Park · beechworthholidaypark.com.au

A bush-park setting on the banks of Spring Creek, 1.5 km from the centre of Beechworth. Level van sites with town water and sullage, a refurbished amenities block, camp kitchen — and movable fire bins with firewood, so we could have our own campfire at the van on a cold July night.

The charm bit The day's drive earns the pub dinner: the Dog on the Tuckerbox at Gundagai, apple country at Batlow, then the long steel spans of the Bethanga Bridge across Lake Hume. And Beechworth itself is the gold-rush town that kept everything — honey-granite streetscape, the courthouse where Ned Kelly was committed for trial, and the famous Beechworth Bakery waiting for breakfast before we roll out.
The day's detours

Batlow — Snowy Valleys apple country, a proper winding back-roads leg well off the Hume.

Bethanga — a tiny Lake Hume village reached over the heritage-listed Bethanga Bridge, one of the great river crossings of the Murray.

Fri3July

Beechworth → Mansfield

~150 km · ~2 hrs via Wangaratta and Benalla — into the High Country
Powered site Mountain views Camp kitchen 17 spacious acres

📍 Mansfield Holiday Park · mansfieldholidaypark.com.au — 8 Timothy Lane, off Mt Buller Rd

Mansfield's big 17-acre park just off Mt Buller Road — large sites spread well apart so we're not crammed in, with rural and mountain views, modern amenities, a campers' kitchen and BBQ area. The shops, cafés and pubs of the main street are only a few minutes away.

The charm bit Winter in the High Country: Mt Buller sitting white on the skyline while we're snug at van level. The Great Victorian Rail Trail starts at the old Mansfield Station — flat, quiet and made for the e-bikes if there's daylight left in the afternoon.
Along the way

Wangaratta — full-size supermarkets for the big top-up before the High Country leg.

Benalla — lakeside art gallery and a good coffee stop roughly halfway.

Sat4July

Mansfield → Geelong

~250 km · ~3¼ hrs · Yea → Melbourne ring road → Geelong
Powered site Barwon River Leafy & quiet 15 min to terminal

📍 Geelong Holiday Park · hampshireholidays.com.au — 153 Barrabool Rd, Belmont

Formerly Barwon River Holiday Park — spacious powered sites, cabins and villas in a leafy, quiet residential pocket of Belmont near the Barwon River. Belmont's shops, supermarkets and cafés are a few minutes away, and Eastern Beach and the Geelong waterfront are only 5–10 minutes up the road.

The charm bit The genius of this one: we wake up already in Geelong. No sailing-morning drive, no watching the clock — just a slow breakfast, the riverside paths along the Barwon for a leg-stretch or an e-bike loop, and a whole unhurried day before an evening sail 15 minutes away.
The run down + last shop

Yea — the classic pie-and-coffee stop as we drop out of the High Country.

The ring road — the M80 skips Melbourne's CBD entirely on the way through to Geelong.

Last big shop: Belmont — supermarkets minutes from the park, but remember: no fruit, veg or honey can come to Tassie, so we shop accordingly.

Sun5July

Sailing day · Geelong

Geelong Holiday Park → Spirit of Tasmania Quay, 136 Corio Quay Rd, North Geelong · ~15 min

We wake up already in Geelong — a whole free day before the evening sail. We'll park up along the waterfront (Eastern Beach has long bays), wander the painted bollards, find a long lunch, and eat down the last of the fresh food. Terminal details: spiritoftasmania.com.au/geelong-terminal

MorningGeelong waterfront — bollard trail, Eastern Beach, a proper long lunch. Emptying the fridge of fruit & veg while we're at it.
−3 hrsFinal van check: gas bottles OFF at the valve, no loose jerry cans of concern, e-ticket + number plate details confirmed.
−2½ hrsCheck-in opens. Drive-up window — we stay in the car. Free parking at the quay for rigs waiting to board.
−2 hrsSweet spot to arrive: through check-in and the biosecurity inspection without queue stress.
−1½ hrsBoarding starts. We grab EVERYTHING we need for the night — no returning to the vehicle deck once we sail.
−45 minCheck-in closes. Hard cutoff — don't test it.
EveningSail. Dinner on board, sleep crossing Bass Strait, wake up in Devonport. 🌊
Biosecurity is the big one: no fruit, vegetables, plants, honey, fish or fish products can go to Tasmania. They inspect at Geelong before boarding (eskies and van fridges get checked), so we've planned meals from Friday onward to run the fresh stuff down to zero.
